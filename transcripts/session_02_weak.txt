So Newton's third law states that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. What that means is that forces always come in pairs. If object A exerts a force on object B, then object B exerts a force back on object A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. This is true always, in every situation, no exceptions. It doesn't matter if one object is moving and the other isn't, it doesn't matter if one is much heavier than the other, it doesn't matter if they're touching or if it's a field force like gravity. The pair is always there. Now the classic example is you push on a wall. You push on the wall with some force, and the wall pushes back on you with exactly the same force. People find this confusing because they say well if the forces are equal why doesn't the wall move, and the answer is that the forces act on different objects, so you can't cancel them. Cancelling only happens when two forces act on the same object. That's the single biggest mistake students make on this topic. Are you with me?

[00:01:49] TUTOR: Good. So let's talk about the horse and cart problem because this comes up on exams a lot. A horse pulls a cart. The cart pulls back on the horse with an equal force. So the question is, how does anything ever move? If they're equal and opposite, shouldn't they cancel and nothing accelerates? And again the answer is the same thing I just said, the forces are on different objects. The horse-on-cart force acts on the cart. The cart-on-horse force acts on the horse. To figure out whether the cart accelerates you only look at the forces on the cart, which is the horse pulling it forward and friction pulling it back. If the horse's pull is bigger than friction, the cart accelerates. The cart-on-horse force is completely irrelevant to the cart's motion because it isn't acting on the cart. Similarly for the horse, you look at the forces on the horse, which is the cart pulling backward and the ground pushing forward on the horse's hooves. So it all works out and there's no paradox, it just looks like a paradox if you forget which object each force is acting on. Does that make sense?
